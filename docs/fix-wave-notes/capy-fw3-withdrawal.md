## Withdrawal: claim-keyed discovery, punctuation-folded fingerprints, no active withdrawn rows

- **Large brains can forget again (#5674, reported by @Grimnoth).** v0.59.10.0 refused every withdrawal once a source passed 12,000 pages, 40,000 chunks, 40,000 facts or 64 MiB of text, checked before the claim was even matched, and queued legacy `source_scan` effects hit the same wall. Discovery is now keyed on the claim, its subject and its fingerprint: the database shortlists pages and chunks by the claim's longest normalized token and its normalized text (every matching row must contain both), discovery streams that shortlist in batches and verifies each row exactly, and a subject-scoped withdrawal reads only its entity's page plus the provenance of that entity's facts. The limits now apply to the claim's affected set (256 pages, 1 MiB manifest, 10 s scan budget). A claim carried by too many pages still refuses loudly, now naming the matched count and sample pages with a recovery path: forget the entity-scoped copies first or edit the claim out of pages that should not carry it. Legacy effects key discovery on the request's own forgotten fact, so a source with more than 256 withdrawals no longer blocks their upgrade.
- **Forgotten facts no longer come back with a period (write-path audit B-9).** Fingerprints fold case, whitespace and sentence punctuation (migration v170), while keeping the symbols that carry meaning in names: `+`, `#` and in-word dots, so "Knows C" never collides with "Knows C++" or "Knows C#", nor "Uses NET" with "Uses .NET". A punctuation, casing or spacing variant re-extracted from unchanged prose stays expired, the snapshot overlay strikes it in fences, and `remember` refuses it. Ledger rows written before v170 keep their exact hash and keep matching; v170 adds a folded row wherever a fact row still holds the claim text, expires facts that became matching, and indexes fact fingerprints for claim lookup. Paraphrases with different words remain a different claim; a semantic (embedding) check is deferred.
- **Fence writers stop writing withdrawn claims as active rows (write-path audit B-10).** `writeFactsToFence` rechecks every input under the page lock and drops claims withdrawn for that page's entity (reporting `withdrawnSkipped`), and the facts backstop skips them in phase 1, so Git, Obsidian and Markdown clones never show a forgotten claim as live.
- **Ported from #5469 by @tarush1989:** the Postgres scale gate (rare, stopword and common-anchor claims each withdraw exactly one page of a 2,500-page source inside a 5 s statement budget) and the structural pin that every coordinated prepare path runs the pre-publication withdrawal recheck before file publication. The recheck wiring itself already shipped on master.
